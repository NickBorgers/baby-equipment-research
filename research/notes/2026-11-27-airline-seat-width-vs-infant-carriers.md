# Airline seat width against three leading infant carriers

## Question

Three leading seats from the market survey have carriers 16.75 in (KeyFit 30), 17 in (Mesa V3) and
17.25 in (Primo Viaggio Nido) wide. Delta and American must publish their
narrowest and widest seat in each class (14 CFR 121.311(k)). Which economy
seats are wide enough? What do the airlines and the FAA say happens when a
carrier does not fit? (Open question 29.)

## Findings

### Seat widths

Width is measured between the inside of the armrests (14 CFR
121.311(k)(2)). For Delta, the narrowest figure is the page's "Child seat
fit guide" row. Its label says "min-max" but it shows one value. On mainline
types that value is lower than the headline width, so it is read here as
the narrowest Main seat. That reading is an inference. For American, the
narrowest figure is the low end of the Main Cabin range.

| Aircraft | Delta narrowest Main (in) | American narrowest Main (in) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A220-100 / -300 | 18.6 | not flown |
| 717 | 18.1 | not flown |
| E170 / E175 | 18.3 | 18.2 |
| E145 | not flown | 17 |
| CRJ-550 / 700 / 900 | 17.3 | CRJ700 16.8, CRJ900 16.6 |
| A319 | 16.3 | 17.2-17.3 (three versions) |
| A320 | 16.3 | 16.5 |
| A321 | 16.3 | 16.3 |
| A321neo | 17.3 (labelled "max") | 16.3 |
| 737-800 | 16.2 | 16.6 |
| 737-900ER | 16.3 | not listed |
| 737 MAX 8 | not flown | 16.6 |
| 757-200 / -300 | 16.2-16.3 | not listed |

Read against the carriers:

- **Wide enough for all three:** Delta A220, 717, E170/E175, CRJs (17.3
  in, only 0.05 in more than the Primo Viaggio); American E170/E175, A319.
- **Narrower than all three:** the narrowest Main seats on Delta's A319,
  A320, A321, 737-800, 737-900ER and 757s (16.2-16.3 in), and on American's
  A320, A321, A321neo, 737-800 and 737 MAX 8 (16.3-16.6 in). The KeyFit
  30's narrower carrier does not get it under any of these.
- These are the **narrowest** seats. The same aircraft list wider Main
  seats (Delta headline 17.2-18 in; American up to 17.8-18.7 in). The
  pages do not say which rows are narrow.
- Delta lists movable armrests mostly on aisle seats only, for example
  "Delta Main: 14 - 29 CD" on the A319. Both airlines prefer the car seat
  in a window seat.

### What happens if it does not fit

- **FAA AC 120-87C para 10.f:** "If an approved CRS, for which a ticket
  has been purchased, does not fit in a particular seat on the aircraft,
  the aircraft operator has the responsibility to accommodate the CRS in
  another seat in the same class of service." 10.f(1): a seat too wide for
  rigid armrests "can be moved to a seat with moveable armrests that can
  be raised".
- **AC 120-87C para 19.c(1):** "Some CRSs will not fit into certain
  aircraft seats. In this instance, the CRS may not be used on those
  seats."
- **American:** "Window seats are the preferred location for a safety
  seat." No exit row, or the rows in front of or behind an overwing exit.
  "Review seat dimensions before your trip." Nothing on width or armrests.
- **Delta:** "The window seat is the preferred location for an approved
  child safety seat". It may go elsewhere if it does not block other row
  occupants from the aisle. Not in exit rows, the rows next to them, or
  rear-facing seats. Nothing on width or armrests.
- **The 16 in figure is not in AC 120-87C.** It is on the FAA's consumer
  page, which returned 403 here. Search snippets show it.

## Confidence

- **High** for the published widths and the policy wording. They come from
  the airlines' own pages and the FAA's AC.
- **Low** for the reading of Delta's single "min-max" value as the
  narrowest seat.
- **Low** for real-world fit. A maker's carrier width is usually its
  widest point. That point is often the handle pivots, which may sit above
  the armrests. No source was found that confirms this for these three
  seats.
- **Medium** overall.

## Candidate impact

- Carrier width barely separates the three seats. All three are wider than
  the narrowest mainline seats. All three fit the regional jets and the
  A220. The Primo Viaggio has the least margin on Delta's CRJs (0.05 in).
- No candidates exist yet. When these seats are added, use this in place
  of "over the FAA's 16 in guide".

## Open questions

- Which rows on each aircraft are the narrow ones? A seat map with widths
  per row, or a call to the airline, would answer it. Book a wider seat,
  or an aircraft type that is wide enough, where possible.
- Where on each carrier is the widest point? Is it at armrest height?
  Measure the chosen carrier at the store.
