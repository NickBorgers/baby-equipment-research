# UPPAbaby Aria V2: installing the carrier without the base

## Question

For the US Aria V2 (1002-ARI): what is the "LAP/SHOULDER EU ROUTING
METHOD", how does it differ from the V1's methods, how does the aircraft
install work, and what happens when the vehicle belt cannot do European
routing? Builds on the
[usability note](2026-11-27-uppababy-aria-v2-usability.md).

## Findings

### What European routing is

The V2 manual allows one carrier-only method in a car (p. 47): "There is
only 1 method of carrier-only installation. LAP/SHOULDER EU ROUTING
METHOD". The steps (pp. 48–50):

1. Level the carrier: "The RED line on the carrier must be level to the
   vehicle floor." A rolled towel may be used.
2. "Route the lap portion of the seat belt through both lap belt guides on
   the carrier, and buckle the seat belt."
3. "Route belt around the seat back through the rear belt guide." The
   p. 49 picture shows the shoulder belt passing behind the shell into the
   "European Vehicle Belt Guide" (parts list item 17).
4. "Lock vehicle seat belt", push the carrier into the seat crease, pull
   the shoulder belt tight, "Feed excess webbing into the vehicle
   retractor."
5. Warning: do not pull out so much shoulder belt that the retractor locks.
   "If it locks, you will have to start over."

Neither manual says "American". That label comes from CPST and research
sources. CChIPS defines the traditional path as "routing the shoulder belt
directly up to the retractor", and the European path as "additionally
wrapping the shoulder belt around the back of the infant carrier."

### What the V1 allowed, and V2 dropped

| V1 method (rev. 240304) | In V2? |
| --- | --- |
| "LAP/SHOULDER PREFFERED ROUTING" — same as V2's EU routing | Yes, the only car method |
| "LAP/SHOULDER OPTIONAL ROUTING" — shoulder belt not wrapped (American style). V1 sent you here "If shoulder belt does not route around the back of the Aria carrier" | **No** |
| "LAP BELT ONLY" — "applies to vehicle with no shoulder belt and aircraft" | **Aircraft only** |

UPPAbaby gives no reason for dropping the other methods. The video
description says only: "It is required that the Aria V2, when installed
using only the vehicle seat belt, follows the European Routing method."

### Aircraft

pp. 52–54 give a separate "AIRCRAFT INSTALLATION METHOD": lap belt through
both front guides, buckled and tightened, no rear wrap. "THIS CHILD SEAT IS
CERTIFIED FOR AIRCRAFT USE WHEN USED WITHOUT A BASE." "DO NOT use base with
aircraft installation." "Inflatable seat belts are not compatible with this
child restraint." "Ask for a seat belt extender if the lap belt is too
short." And: "Never use the AIRCRAFT SEATBELT PATH in a car."

So "only 1 method" is true for cars only. This is a wording inconsistency
in the manual, not an instruction conflict.

### Where it fails

- **Short shoulder belt.** The p. 49 banner says: "If you are having
  difficulty routing the shoulder belt behind the Aria carrier, contact
  Customer Service 1 (844) 823-3132, see page 50". p. 50 has no such
  guidance. There is no fallback routing.
- **Lap-only belt in a car.** No carrier-only install is possible
  (inference from pp. 47 and 53). V1 allowed it.
- **Inflatable vehicle belts.** p. 27 bans "Inflatable" belts for the
  restraint as a whole, with or without the base.
- **Field reports: not found.** No owner, CPST or forum report was found
  of European routing failing on an Aria in a taxi, rideshare, rental car
  or Model Y. Reddit was blocked, so this is weak evidence of absence.

### How likely a short belt is (general, not Aria V2)

- CChIPS, 30-vehicle convenience sample: "generally good compatibility
  across the field"; the belt had to be "extended to nearly its maximum
  length".
- CSFTL (V1): "the Euro routing installation didn't actually use all of my
  seat belt". She measured the V1 carrier at "roughly 23 inches" long.
  The maker lists V2 at 25.8 in long against V1 24.5 in (see the
  [fit update](2026-11-28-uppababy-aria-v2-hands-on-and-fit-update.md)),
  so V2 may need a little more belt (inference).
- The Car Seat Lady: belts that "seem too short" are "almost all … truly
  long enough if you do the tipping trick". Safe Ride News: buckle after
  wrapping, not before. **The V2 manual says buckle first** (p. 49 step 1),
  and says nothing about the tipping trick. Either trick departs from the
  manual (inference).

### Why European routing is said to be more stable

- CChIPS: "the European belt path appeared to create a more stable
  installation compared to the traditional method, especially when misuses
  were present"; "caregivers had fewer major errors using the European belt
  path (43.3%) compared to the traditional belt path (80.0%)".
- The Car Seat Lady (experience, not data): the European path gives a
  secure install "in about 95% of vehicles", the American path "in only
  about 20% of cars in the US". "The European belt path does for the
  carrier installation what the load leg does for a base installation."
- Safe Ride News: the wrap "limits forward rotation", but "there is no
  required U.S. test to measure the effectiveness of installations using
  Euro-routing".

### Manual revision changes (250904 → 260724)

- Expiry: 7 → 10 years.
- Footcover: 250904 said "USE ONLY on stroller. DO NOT use in vehicle".
  260724 drops that and adds "Confirm vehicle belt is installed through
  belt guides and under the footcover." Owners with the older printed
  manual read a different rule.
- Cross-references are off in 260724: "see page 50" (p. 49) and "REVIEW
  SAFETY CHECK ON PAGE 54" (the check is on p. 55).

## Confidence

- **High** for the manual text and the conclusions drawn directly from it:
  one car method, a separate lap-only aircraft method, no carrier-only
  route with lap-only or inflatable vehicle belts.
- **Medium** that European routing is more stable and less error-prone:
  one industry-sponsored study summary, plus CPST and maker statements. No
  test of the Aria V2 itself.
- **Medium-low** that short belts rarely block it in practice: none of the
  evidence is for the V2.
- **Low / none** for real taxi, rideshare, rental or Model Y results.
