# Peg Perego Primo Viaggio (Nido and variants): usability

## Question

Usability pass for the Primo Viaggio family sold in the US now (2025
manuals, 4-30 lb): Nido first, then part by part for the Primo Viaggio,
Lounge and Urban Mobility. This also answers open question 36: re-read the
sources behind the summary Nick shared, and confirm, refute or qualify
each of its six claims.

## Findings

### Which version each review tested

**No hands-on review of the current 4-30 Nido was found.** Every usability
finding below is from a 4-35 Nido.

| Source | Tested | Same as the current Nido? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CSFTL 2017 | first 4-35 Nido; base with a "recline foot"; no Kinetic Pods mentioned | different generation |
| Car Seat Lady ©2019 | 4-35 and 4-35 Nido, "the same carrier except the Nido has a larger sun canopy" | different generation |
| BabyGearLab 2023 | "Primo Viaggio 4-35 Nido", 35 lb maximum | unclear: original (IMPV04) or Nido K (IMPV07) |
| CR m419413 | titled "Primo Viaggio Nido", 4-30 lb | its pros, cons and Take match the 4-35 page word for word; inference: results carried over from the 4-35 test |

**What the "Nido K" is.** Retailers describe it as the "4-35 Nido K… now
with Kinetic Pods". Kidsland USA lists it in the US with MPN IMPV07US00MB53.
The 2025 Nido manual gives "Product reference number IMPV07•" and describes
Kinetic Pods (p.51). Inference, medium-low confidence: the current US Nido
is the Nido K renamed, with the stated maximum cut to 30 lb. Retail listings
for IMPV07 still say 35 lb; the manual says "4 - 30 lbs", and the manual
wins. Canada still sells a 4-35 Nido.

### The six claims in the shared summary

| Claim | Verdict | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recline indicator cannot be trusted | **Not supported as worded.** The bubble is easy to read. The problems are the weight zones and reachability. | below |
| Poor insert fit | **Confirmed for the 2017 version only.** | CSFTL 2017 |
| Lock-off does not hold | **Confirmed by one lab, disputed by two CPST sources.** | BabyGearLab vs CSFTL, Car Seat Lady |
| Click-in indicator can show green when not locked | **Confirmed for the 2017 base; not retested.** | CSFTL 2017 |
| Stiff buckle | **Confirmed, one source.** | BabyGearLab 2023 |
| As-used carrier 10.6-11 lb vs 8.2 lb | **Confirmed, with caveats** on what each figure includes. | below |

### Getting it right every time

**Recline.**

- BabyGearLab 2023: "The level indicator is a bubble style that settles
  quickly and is easy to read." The knob adjustment "takes forever."
- CSFTL 2017: "The Nido base's recline angle is based on the weight of the
  baby." Peg Perego told CSFTL "the recline bubble is just a guide" and a
  newborn should have more recline. CSFTL: "This lack of clarity could
  result in parents putting their newborns in a more upright and dangerous
  position."
- Car Seat Lady: three zones, 4-11, 12-22 and 23-35 lb. "The most reclined
  range (the 4-11 pound range) can be used for ALL babies."
- 2025 Nido manual p.27 still says to "set the angle corresponding to the
  weight of your child." The 4-30 base's zones are in an image and were
  not read.
- Reachability: CSFTL (2017 Honda Civic) "could not get the recline bubble
  much past the borderline between the two lower ranges". A pool noodle
  gained "a minuscule amount". CR con: "Insufficient recline adjustment for
  newborns in some vehicles." BabyGearLab reached it, slowly.
- **The two 2025 manuals disagree about the same base.** The Nido manual
  mentions a towel or noodle only for the baseless install (p.39). The
  Urban Mobility manual allows "a rolled towel or pool noodle under the
  base" (p.39). Peg Perego told CSFTL in 2017 it would add the noodle to
  the manual.
- **Misuse risk:** following the weight zones can set a heavier newborn
  more upright than Peg Perego told CSFTL is right. In flat-seated cars the
  newborn angle may be out of reach, and the Nido manual offers no noodle
  for the base.
- Model Y reachability: not found.

**Inserts and harness fit (CSFTL 2017, hands-on).**

- Newborn doll: with stage 1, "the crotch buckle was below the knees";
  without it, "we had head slump."
- 10 weeks, 13 lb, 24 in: "She fits beautifully with the stage 2 insert in."
- 10 weeks, 13 lb, 22 in, long torso: head slump without stage 1; buckle
  sides under the knees with it.
- 4 months, 16 lb: "With the insert, the sides of the buckle dug into his
  thighs. Without the insert, his chin was pressed toward his chest."
- 7 months, 18 lb: outgrew stage 2, "the sides of the buckle dug far into
  his thighs. His head is still a bit slumped without any of the inserts."
- 9 months, 21 lb: "fits well in the seat without either insert."
- BabyGearLab 2023 ran no age-by-age fit. The insert fabric is "the
  roughest".
- Whether today's inserts are the same parts as 2017's: not found.

**What the manuals say about inserts.**

- 2017 manual (quoted by CSFTL): stage 1 "until around 8 pounds".
- 2025 Nido, Primo Viaggio and Urban Mobility: the dual-stage system "may
  be used from birth up to about 20 lbs (9 kg) or when the torso of your
  baby fully occupies the space between the crotch strap and the shoulder
  slots". No separate rule for removing stage 1.
- 2025 Lounge manual: "about 8 pounds (3,6 kg)" for the same system.
- Peg Perego told CSFTL stage 1 "could be used as long as needed". Car
  Seat Lady says remove it by about 8 lb.
- **Misuse risk:** the current manual does not say clearly when to remove
  stage 1, and the Nido and Lounge manuals disagree (20 lb vs 8 lb).

**Base install hardware.**

- Lock-off. BabyGearLab 2023, LATCH: "our lock-off wouldn't stay locked
  and didn't provide any tensioning." Belt: "we could pull on the belt,
  and it popped right open… We had to pull the belt and engage the car
  retractor before using the lock-off." CSFTL 2017 and Car Seat Lady
  describe it removing a little slack. The 2025 manual also has you lock
  the vehicle belt (ALR or locking clip, p.32).
- **Misuse risk** (one unit, 2023): a lock-off that pops open, with an
  unlocked retractor, gives a loose install. The manual's ALR step reduces
  this.
- No tensioner (BabyGearLab).
- Load leg. BabyGearLab: "so bothersome and strangely designed that we
  wondered if ours was broken… doesn't slide out on its own or provide a
  color change." CSFTL: "smooth and intuitive", but "difficult to extend
  that 'one last click'."
- Load-leg indicator. The 2025 manual has a "Leg deployment indicator"
  that shows the leg is deployed. It does not show that the foot touches
  the floor ("The correct adjustment of the foot is when the foot touches
  the floor"). BabyGearLab 2023: "there is no indicator when you get it
  right." Whether the 2023 unit had the deployment indicator: not found.

**Click-in indicator.**

- CSFTL 2017: "If the carrier is resting on the base but not securely
  clicked in, the indicator may still show green. Always, always pull up
  firmly on the handle… Do not rely on the indicator alone."
- BabyGearLab 2023 reported no false green.
- 2025 manual p.37: press until the click, "You will not be able to move
  the car seat if it is properly locked in place", then check the
  indicators are green. It does not explicitly say to pull up on the
  handle.
- **Misuse risk:** the indicator can give false assurance. Whether the
  current base fixed this: not found.

**Baseless install.**

- 2025 manual: European routing, or "only the lap portion of the belt in
  the handle slots" if the belt is too short (p.40). CSFTL's "American
  routing" is not in the 2025 manual.
- BabyGearLab: the easiest method, but "we almost ran out of vehicle belt
  length… The seat feels very wobbly."
- CR carrier-only fit: 3/5. Car Seat Lady: "some seat belts will be too
  short."

### Parts shared across the four US variants (2025 manuals)

| Part | Nido | Lounge | Primo Viaggio | Urban Mobility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carrier shell | Kinetic Pods | Kinetic Pods; **reclining backrest**, must be "MOST UPRIGHT" in a vehicle | Kinetic Pods | Kinetic Pods; **LATCH built into the shell** |
| Harness, buckle, handle | same manual text in all four; hardware identity likely shared (inference) | same | same | same |
| Inserts | "about 20 lbs" | "**about 8 pounds**" | "about 20 lbs" | "about 20 lbs" |
| Base | load leg, anti-rebound bar, red/green indicator, leg indicator, knob, lock-off | same as Nido | **no load leg, no red/green indicator**; knob, lock-off, anti-rebound bar | sold without a base; takes either |

So the Nido and Lounge base findings apply to each other. For the plain
Primo Viaggio base, the lock-off finding may apply; the indicator and load
leg findings do not.

### Where the maker and its manuals disagree

- Pool noodle under the base: promised to CSFTL; in the Urban Mobility
  manual; missing from the Nido manual.
- Weight zones: "just a guide" to CSFTL; still the instruction in the
  manual.
- Stage 1 insert: "as long as needed" to CSFTL; no stage-1 rule in the
  manual.
- Lower-anchor borrowing: allowed per CSFTL 2017; the 2025 manual p.15 says
  "Peg Perego does not allow 'borrowing' lower anchors."

### Also noticed

- CR rates the Urban Mobility's crash protection "Basic": "the seat did not
  maintain its structural integrity in any of our test conditions." This is
  a lab review, not a certification finding.
- The 2025 Nido manual's crash-check page names "Primo Viaggio Urban
  Mobility", and its logic is garbled ("If any one of these five
  statements apply, the seat may be damaged").

## Confidence

- **High** for what the 2025 manuals say and how the variants differ.
- **Medium-low** that the current Nido is the Nido K renamed (retailer SKUs
  and the manual's product code, no maker statement).
- **Low to medium** for every usability finding: one unit per lab, 2017 or
  2023, all on the 4-35 Nido, none retested on the 4-30. The LATCH door and
  recline reachability each have two sources. The lock-off and buckle each
  have one, and the lock-off finding is contradicted.
- **Medium** that the as-used carrier is about 10-11 lb.

## Candidate impact

Nothing yet: no seat is shortlisted. If a Primo Viaggio variant is
shortlisted, its `car-seat` item gets:

- cons starting "Misuse risk:" for the weight-zone recline guidance, the
  unclear stage-1 insert rule, the click-in indicator that showed green
  unlatched (2017), and the lock-off that popped open (2023, disputed);
- daily-friction cons for the stiff buckle, hard release from the base,
  and fragile LATCH door;
- carrier weight as used about 10-11 lb, not the maker's 8.2 lb;
- a note that every review tested the 4-35 Nido, not the current 4-30.

The Urban Mobility also gets CR's "Basic" crash rating as a con, labeled
as a lab review.
