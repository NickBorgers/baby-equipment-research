## Question

Follow-up to the [usability note](2026-11-27-uppababy-aria-v2-usability.md)
for the US Aria V2 (1002-ARI). Is there a CPST hands-on review? A measured
headrest height? Fit by age, and which limit ends use? Defects, recalls,
NHTSA complaints? New owner reports on the base tightness indicator and
the lock-off?

## Findings

### CPST hands-on review: still none

Safe in the Seat lists the V2 as "Review Coming Soon" (2026-11-24). CSFTL
has only the V1. The only new V2 hands-on source with a safety-program
link is the Texas A&M KidSafe install demo, with no baby:

- Base, vehicle belt with the lock-off: "Nothing side to side. Nothing
  forwards and backwards, and our level indicator … is within the limits."
- European routing: "No movement, super safe."
- The level indicator "has two zones, one for 0 to 4 months, and then an
  acceptable range for after their 4 months."

### Headrest height: not measured anywhere

Maker outside dimensions (comparison sheets):

| Carrier | Sheet | L × W × H | Height limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aria V1 | 250513 | 24.5 × 17 × 18.5 in | 30 in |
| Aria V2 | 260202, 260827 | 25.8 × 17 × 23.7 in | 30 in |
| Mesa V3 | 260827 | 25.8 × 17 × 23 in | 32 in |

- These are outside dimensions with no stated method. The 5.2 in height
  jump from V1 to V2 is probably a different measuring method, not a
  taller shell (inference).
- Retailers say "2 inches longer" (Bambi Baby, no baseline). The maker's
  own figures show 1.3 in more length than V1.
- The 30 in limit did not change. A longer shell can add leg room but
  cannot extend use past 30 in (inference). Whether the headrest top is
  higher, so the 1 in head-room rule runs out later, is unmeasured.
- Maker: both the Aria V2 and the Mesa V3 are for "Approximately 0–1
  years". Help center: "The Mesa is perfect for families looking for added
  longevity as the seat accommodates babies up to 32”."

### Fit by age (owner reports, low confidence)

Most of this evidence is one seeded group: the "Stellar Product Testing
Panel", who got free seats at launch. The same texts appear on UPPAbaby,
Target, Nordstrom and Babylist. Agreement among panel testers is not
independent agreement.

**Correction to the 2026-11-27 note:** the "outgrow this seat by her 9m
bday" review is a panel tester's (an incentivized copy ends "Thanks to the

is from an unpaid owner.

No report names the limit that ended use (head room, 30 in, or 30 lb).

### Defects, recalls, NHTSA

- **NHTSA (primary):** "UPPABABY | ARIA" has 0 recalls, 0 investigations,
  1 complaint. NHTSA ID 11760538, filed 2026-08-29, no crash or injury:
  "Fabric was washed one time in the washer and the sharp Velcro stuck to
  the back of the fabric, ripping it. Brand new aria v2 car seat!"
- **NHTSA ease-of-use ratings:** still no Aria of either version.
- **Owner, unpaid, 2026-06-22 (Babylist):** "One of the mechanisms that is
  supposed to "lock" the car seat in place was stuck on one side and would
  not descend." The part is not named.
- **Canopy:** still the single 2026-09-07 report.
- **Footcover comes off:** five reports, four panel and one unpaid ("you
  put it back on more than it's worth fighting"). Daily friction, not a
  defect.
- **Handle:** buttons "stiff", "always seem to stick"; handle "too thin
  and digs into my arm" (panel).

### Base indicators and lock-off

- Tightness indicator falls back to red after driving: "it would still go
  from green to red after I drive" (unpaid, already recorded); "after
  nearly every car ride, my base indicator is back to red or in the middle
  of red and green. This may be a user issue" (panel). BabyGearLab found
  the opposite symptom, green too early. Both point to the indicator being
  unreliable as the only check (inference).
- Lock-off: BabyGearLab V2 found it did not hold the belt well. The KidSafe
  demo got no movement with it in one vehicle. **The V2 evidence now
  conflicts.**
- Fire-station installs: one needed "a little effort and foam" in a 2020
  Honda Pilot (unpaid). The manual allows a rolled towel; foam may not
  follow the manual (unverified). Another needed a towel in a 2024 Acura
  MDX (panel).

## Confidence

- **High** for the NHTSA record and the maker's dimensions and limits.
- **Medium-low** for the lock-off: one lab and one demo disagree.
- **Low** for fit by age, the indicator falling back to red, and each
  defect report.
- **Not found:** CPST hands-on review, measured headrest height, fit at
  9–12 months.
