# The US stroller standard: 16 CFR 1227 and ASTM F833-21

## Question

Open question 16, and the standard part of 28. Which edition of ASTM F833
does 16 CFR 1227 incorporate now, and what does it say about stability
with a car seat attached, brakes, newborn recline and labels?

## Findings

### Edition

eCFR § 1227.2: "Each carriage and stroller shall comply with all
applicable provisions of ASTM F833-21, Standard Consumer Safety
Performance Specification for Carriages and Strollers, approved June 15,
2021." Mandatory from **2022-02-15** (86 FR 64345). No later change in
eCFR. ASTM opened a revision, WK90686, on 2024-05-09: "New Task Group is
chartered with the task of evaluating CPSC data from stroller incidents."
No ballot is shown.

| CFR edition | Rule | Effective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F833-13b (with a CPSC change) | 79 FR 13208 | 2015-09-10 |
| F833-15 | 81 FR 37128 | 2016-10-02 |
| F833-19 | 84 FR 37763 | 2019-11-05 |
| F833-21 | 86 FR 64345 | 2022-02-15 |

### Definitions (F833-21 text)

- 3.1.18 **reclined carriage position**: "adjustment position of a
  convertible carriage/stroller that allows for an interior angle of
  greater than 150° between the seat surface and the backrest."
- 3.1.23 stroller: "wheeled vehicle for the transport of infants or
  children generally in a sitting-up or semi-reclined position. …
  Strollers normally are used for children from infancy to 36 months of
  age."
- 3.1.10 infant: "young child who is not capable of sitting unassisted."
- 5.11 Seat Recline Measurement: "Place the back of the stroller in the
  most reclined position. Place the hinged boards with the hinged edge
  into the junction of the stroller back and seat (see Fig. 6). Measure
  the angle between the two boards."

### A newborn recline rule: not found

The visible text defines how to measure recline and what counts as a
carriage position (over 150°). No source read shows a minimum recline or
an age rule for a seat used from birth. CPSC's warning table lists an
angle-and-age warning for infant swings, but none for strollers. That
suggests F833 has no such rule. This is an inference, not proof. So
"suitable from birth" is the maker's own claim, not a tested threshold
we can point to.

### A car seat on a stroller ("combination unit")

- 78 FR 29279 (describing F833-13): "ASTM F833-13 requires combination
  units to meet general requirements associated with latching, parking
  brakes, static load, and stability and tip over."
- 81 FR 37128 (F833-15): "aside from the restraint system, the
  combination unit of a car seat on a stroller must still comply with all
  of the other applicable requirements when the car seat is installed in
  all of the manufacturer's recommended use positions."
- 86 FR 64345 (F833-21) adds the impact test (6.7.1) and the
  head-entrapment test (6.10) for combination units.

### Test figures: older editions only

These come from CPSC's summary of **F833-08**. Later editions made the
tests stricter, and the current figures were not readable.

- Stability: "The product with a 17-lbm. CAMI dummy shall not tip over
  when placed on a 12[deg] incline and shall not tip forward when a 40 lb.
  force is applied downward where a child would likely step to climb into
  the stroller." F833-13 made it "more stringent", with no numbers given.
- Impact (combination unit): "the car seat may not become completely
  separated from the frame, with 40 lb. (or maximum recommended weight)
  secured by the restraint system in each seating area, then allowed to
  roll 40 in. down a 20[deg] slope into a rigid steel stop."
- Brakes: "the braked wheels shall not rotate more than 90[deg] when
  tested on a 12[deg] incline". F833-13 raised the force by about 50%.
  F833-21 tests on 60-grit sandpaper and adds rules so a seated child
  cannot release the brake.
- Static load: "A combination unit of a car seat on a stroller must
  support a 50-lb. weight".

Not found: whether the stability test with a car seat attached loads a
dummy into the car seat, and any limit on how high the carrier may sit.

### Labels

CPSC Resellers Guide p. 68 (F833-19): "The retail product package, if
provided, shall state the maximum weight of the intended user." For
products with a reclined carriage position: "Child may slip into leg
openings and strangle. Never use in reclined carriage position(s) unless
(manufacturer to insert product specific instructions)."

### Stroller bassinets and sleep rules

CPSC recall 24719 (Peg Perego bassinets, 2023-11-30) cites the Safe
Sleep for Babies Act and the Infant Sleep Products rule. So a stroller
bassinet marketed for sleep is judged against those rules as well as
F833.

## Confidence

- **High:** the edition, its date, the edition history, and the F833-21
  definitions (read from the standard's own text in a partial sample).
- **Medium:** that combination units must pass latching, brakes, static
  load, stability and impact (CPSC states it, describing F833-13 and
  F833-15).
- **Low as a description of today's standard:** the test numbers, which
  describe F833-08.

## Candidate impact

Nothing changes in the candidates. F833-21 is mandatory for every
stroller sold in the US, but `safety.standards` stays empty until a
maker's own page or manual is read for each item. Do not fill it from
this note.

Two things for the recommendation:

- "From birth" is the maker's claim. A measured recline (e.g.
  BabyGearLab's 157.7° for the Minu V3) is the only check we have. Over
  150° is the standard's carriage threshold, not a newborn rule.
- The standard does not limit how high a carrier sits. Open question 28
  stays a usability question.
