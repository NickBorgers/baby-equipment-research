# 0003 — No rotating infant seat; rotation goes in the next seat, and the stroller outlasts the infant seat

## Context

[0002](0002-infant-car-seat-that-clicks-onto-the-stroller.md) chose an
infant seat now and a second seat later. Two questions followed (Nick,
2026-11-27):

- **Rotation.** There was interest in a rotating seat, to make getting the
  baby in and out easier. Nick expected it to conflict with portability.
- **Rebuying.** Nick would "prefer to not rebuy everything later".

The research
([rotation note](../research/notes/2026-11-27-rotating-infant-car-seats.md),
[market survey](../research/notes/2026-11-27-infant-car-seat-market-survey.md))
found:

- All four rotating infant seats on the US market (Cybex Aton G2 Swivel,
  Maxi-Cosi Peri 180, Joie Rue LX Spin, Graco SnugRide Turn & Slide) rotate
  in the **base**. The carrier weighs the same or nearly the same as the
  fixed version, flies without the base, and clicks onto the same strollers.
  So rotation does **not** conflict with portability.
- The swivel costs a heavier base (14-17 lb against about 9 lb), about
  $90-150 more per seat, and works only in our own car.
- **No rotating infant seat has a clean explicit FMVSS 213a claim**, which
  [0001](0001-car-seat-must-meet-fmvss-213a.md) requires. Graco's was
  recalled in April 2026 because its carrier could detach from the base.
- Most rotating seats are convertibles or all-in-ones, which stay in the car.

## Decision

1. **The infant seat does not rotate.** Rotation is not a criterion for it.
2. **Rotation is a requirement for the follow-on seat**, the one bought when
   the infant seat is outgrown. That seat should also be the last one if
   possible: one that runs from the infant seat's end through as many years
   as its expiry allows (for example an all-in-one that ends as a booster).
3. **The stroller is chosen to outlast the infant seat.** It must work for a
   newborn on its own (lie-flat or near-flat seat, or its own bassinet),
   carry a toddler (to about 50 lb), and take the chosen infant seat through
   a manufacturer-listed adapter. When the infant seat is outgrown, the
   adapters are retired, not the stroller.

## Rationale

- **Rotation helps most after the infant stage.** This is our judgment, not a
  sourced finding. With a carrier, the baby is usually buckled in outside
  the car, and the swivel only eases clicking the carrier into the base.
  With a seat that stays in the car, the child is lifted in and buckled
  there on every trip, for years. That is also where the market puts its
  rotating seats.
- **It removes a conflict with 0001.** No rotating infant seat qualifies
  cleanly today, so wanting one would mean relaxing 0001 or waiting on makers.
- **It keeps the rebuy to the car seat.** With 2 and 3, the whole run is two
  car seats and one stroller. What is thrown away when the infant stage ends
  is the infant base and the stroller adapters, roughly $150-250 of parts.
  The infant seat and base can be kept for a later child until they expire.

## Alternatives considered

- **A rotating infant seat now.** It lost because none meets 0001 cleanly,
  it costs more, and it helps less than rotation does later. Rotation's
  conflict with portability turned out not to be real.
- **A stroller picked to suit the infant seat** — a car seat frame stroller,
  which has no seat of its own. It lost because it ends when the infant seat
  ends, which is the rebuy Nick wants to avoid.
- **A car seat with built-in wheels (Doona).** It is still an infant seat
  under 0002, but its stroller ends with it, so a lasting stroller is needed
  anyway. That removes most of its advantage. It is not shortlisted.
- **A follow-on seat that is not the last seat** (a convertible that is
  later followed by a separate booster). Not ruled out, but it is a third
  purchase. Prefer one that runs to the booster stage if a good one meets
  0001 and fits the Model Y.

## Consequences

- **Infant seat:** rotating seats come off the shortlist. Open question 33
  (missing rotating seat specs) no longer blocks anything now.
- **Stroller:** a candidate must be newborn-capable without the car seat,
  rated to about 50 lb, and take the chosen infant seat. Frame strollers are
  out. This favors infant seats that fit many strollers (the Peg Perego Primo
  Viaggio fits the widest range in the survey), so the stroller can be
  chosen on its own merits.
- **Follow-on seat:** must rotate, meet 0001, and fit the Model Y.
  Its expiry should cover the years it is meant for. It is not researched
  now. It is researched when the infant seat is close to outgrown (open
  question 27).
- **Model Y room for rotation** moves to the follow-on seat (open question
  30): a rotating convertible needs room to swing in the outboard seat.
